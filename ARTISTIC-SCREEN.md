# ひらく鉤版 — local v0.19 candidate

`artisticscreen` is one original, non-nested hook family: an ink hook develops into connected curved bands, then into a paper hook cut out of ink. This local candidate adds two looks (72 effects / 89 looks / 62 recipe files). Code and visual review retain it as a scoped local candidate; it is not a published release or an assertion of user aesthetic acceptance.

## Source and interpretation

[Ostromoukhov & Hersch, Artistic Screening, SIGGRAPH 1995](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/ostrom/publications/pdf/SIGGRAPH95_ArtisticScreening.pdf), especially sections 2–3 and figures 6, 9–11, motivates a separate shape for each tone, including changes impossible with a single fixed threshold matrix. The paper's corresponding Bézier contour interpolation, contour editor, nonlinear mappings, device calibration and microletter applications are not reproduced here. No paper artwork or code is included.

Our authored family blends three geometric distance fields (open circular hook, paired corner arcs, reversed displaced hook). Each of 257 tone states is independently area-calibrated on 64×64 equal-area texels: sort that state's geometric scores, with raster-index ties, and fill exactly `16 * step` texels. States are never sorted by area or reordered. This is an original raster-area construction, not the paper's interpolation method or a gamma correction guarantee. The transition control changes the geometry weights at a given ink coverage, not the requested coverage.

At transition 50, state 51 has 816 ink texels and state 128 has 2048. Of the earlier ink, 164 texels disappear while 1396 new ones appear. All 257 authored states have strictly increasing measured area. At transitions 20 / 50 / 80, consecutive-state removals total 886 / 927 / 673 texels respectively. Topology changes alone are not the evidence of non-nesting; these removals are.

The existing halftone operator also relocates ink when it switches between central dots and corner holes around its middle tone. Non-nesting alone is therefore not new within this app. The candidate is retained for its authored hook/band/counterform sequence and equal-area transition control, not for a claim that no current effect can remove earlier ink.

## Effect contract

- Stable defaults: size 22, angle 30°, transition 50, ink 0, paper 100; shared engine defaults are unchanged. Size is relative to a 1000-pixel long edge. There is no nonlinear coordinate warp.
- Rotation and uniform scale form an affine square lattice. Origin is image upper-left `(0,0)`; `floor` gives half-open cell ownership, including negative lattice coordinates. Seed is unused by this effect and its catalog is nonrandomized. Shared grain preprocessing can still use seed.
- Input to the effect is the engine's already-preprocessed, opaque byte raster. Decode sRGB channels, use linear luminance weights 0.2126 / 0.7152 / 0.0722, and average each cell with 4×4 midpoint samples per contributing input pixel. Partial cells average only their visible sampled support. One constant tone is selected per cell, rounded to the nearest of 257 states.
- Requested ink fraction is `(paperLinear - cellLuminance) / (paperLinear - inkLinear)`, clamped to [0,1]. UI bounds keep ink and paper distinct. The colors are one neutral ink and one neutral paper, controlled by their sRGB brightness percentages; out-of-range source tones clip to these endpoints.
- Each output pixel uses 4×4 samples of the selected binary masks. Ink source-over paper is averaged in linear light, then encoded to sRGB. At cell boundaries each sample belongs to one cell; holes contribute **paper**, never transparent pixels. Half black / half white coverage produces byte 188. All output alpha bytes are 255.
- Common saturation, monochrome, brightness, contrast and grain run before this effect; common inversion, monochrome and mix run afterward. The engine's shared alpha flattening and mix operate on encoded bytes. No linear-light or one-ink guarantee is extended to the full pipeline when those operations change it.
- Same input, dimensions and parameters are deterministic. Preview and export can receive differently resampled input; equal spatial units do not imply identical bytes at different resolutions.

## Intended use and visible limitations

`hook-weave` (size 16, transition 50, 30°, brightness +12) preserves more photographic structure. `open-counterform` (size 30, transition 65, 15°, brightness +12) makes the repeating shapes prominent, with substantial detail loss. The default lies between them. Existing UI controls and recipe import/export are used unchanged.

The white calla's lit petal becomes a connected band while darker areas retain paper hooks. The color bouquet becomes a monochrome figure with strong local repetition; its original color separation is lost. Architecture is the weakest case: the lattice competes with windows and fine railings. The larger look is deliberately coarse and should not be treated as a detail-preserving photo conversion. Thin synthetic bridges and letter counters can disappear or break. No human blind study or physical print assessment was performed.

Comparison against halftone, inksearch, score and score→halftone used the same three bundled photographs at 900px long edge. Candidate/halftone/score nominal size was 22. Inksearch density 6 uses a power-of-two 32×64 or 64×32 grid: its portrait pitch is 18.75×14.0625px versus the candidate's rotated 19.8px square pitch, so it is not a pitch-identical comparison. Final-stage brightness was tuned against measured mean linear luminance; inksearch also used ink 45, 85 iterations and spread 115; score→halftone used final contrast 60 and dot size 7. Mean errors were below 0.005 for these three tuned alternatives. Score alone remained 0.19–0.26 lighter in linear luminance within the tested controls. Its paper is 244 and ink depends on input, so exact endpoint matching is unavailable. These residual differences prevent a universal ranking; score already changes glyph topology with tone.

Independent follow-up also tested score with paper retention 60, size 16 and weight 80, against a size-16 candidate with brightness +20 / contrast 105. Final brightness tuning brought score mean linear luminance within 0.0024 on the two flowers and 0.0059 on architecture; thus the earlier lighter score result is not its strongest reachable setting. This score setting keeps more photographic detail but uses source-dependent grays, so it does not share fixed black/white endpoints. A finer score→halftone combination was also competitive for detail. The candidate retains stronger positive/negative hook shapes; it is not a general winner. Inksearch density 6 was checked in this follow-up too, with 15.625 normalized long-edge spacing against the candidate size 16 (not an exact square-grid match).

A same-source look refinement compared brightness 0 / +12 / +20 across all three photographs. +12 was retained for the fine look: it opens the bouquet and architecture without the larger bright area of +20 on the calla. This is a provisional model design judgment, not user preference.

Small output cells alias. A sweep of all 257 requested tones on uniform 32×32 inputs, angle 0, found maximum coverage error about 0.202 / 0.040 / 0.024 / 0.012 / 0.0042 / 0.0041 at pitches 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 pixels. The first four pitches had tone reversals; the last two had none in this sweep. Input/output byte quantization is included. This does **not** establish monotonicity at every angle, phase, transition or partial boundary. Authored 64×64 area monotonicity is distinct from finite output sampling. Prefer readable cells around 16px or larger when the hook opening matters; even there very thin extreme-tone holes may disappear. The 64×64 mask itself limits enlarged contour smoothness.

A separate diagnostic fed linear-luminance bytes to halftone to reduce the legacy transfer-function mismatch. This preprocessing is a test fixture, not a new shipped UI feature. It brought mean errors to 0.0036 / 0.00025 / 0.00083 for color / calla / architecture and showed that tonal representation alone is not a new capability. The relevant distinction remains the hooked and connected forms. Changing only the candidate transition 25→50→75 changed the visible bands while mean linear luminance varied by at most 0.00017 across these photographs.

## Local checks

125 tests pass, including nine new tests for measured authored area/non-nesting, equal-area geometry changes, linear-light AA, a hand-calculated partial-cell boundary pixel, independent full-cell color/mean limits, exact same-size restoration/seed independence, tiny/partial/rotated cells, engine alpha/mix/monochrome, historical defaults and all 62 recipe assets. All 71 prior default outputs match the baseline hashes on the 48×64 fixture. `tests/v018-catalog.json` preserves IDs, look IDs and exact defaults; old engine tags reject the new effect and still accept their existing effects.

The local shell had Node 24.19.0 but no `npm` on PATH. The package-script equivalents were run directly: `node --test tests/*.test.js`; Vite build with `--configLoader native` (default config bundling hit a sandbox directory-read error); `node scripts/write-build-info.mjs`; `node scripts/check-release.mjs`. Build and release-path checks passed. No lint script was added.

At 600×900 / 900×600, the two integrated looks took 356–555ms in local Node measurements (single runs, mixed cold/warm family cache). These are neither phone timings nor a 4096px throughput promise. Real Safari, phone performance, physical print, long-duration maximum load and user aesthetic adoption remain unverified.

Installed Microsoft Edge 154.0.4258.48, headless Playwright, production preview at 1440×1000 and 390×844: selected `hook-weave`, changed transition 50→25→50 and 50→75→50 on both widths, and restored the SHA-256 of all canvas RGBA bytes each time. Original comparison worked. The decoded exported PNG and preview were both 1024×1536; direct comparison of 6,291,456 RGBA bytes found zero differences. The export/readback check took 1.41s in that run. Console warnings/errors and horizontal overflow were zero after fixing a readback warning caused by the test harness's canvas access. These are desktop Edge results at two viewport widths, not mobile-device or Safari verification.

## Final local review check

After the fine look was changed to brightness +12, the complete 125-test suite passed again under Node 24.19.0. The production build and release-path checks also passed in the outer environment. Installed Edge 154.0.4258.48 was driven from Node 22.22.3 at 1440×1000 and 390×844: each transition 50→25→50 and 50→75→50 restored identical full pixel hashes. The final 1024×1536 PNG compared all 6,291,456 RGBA bytes against the same-size preview with zero differences; console errors/warnings, overlays and horizontal overflow were zero. This is a local check, not a deployment or a different-resolution equivalence claim.

Final two-look measurements at 600×900 / 900×600 were 460–590ms per single render in Node 24.19.0, with mixed cache state and other verification work on the host. Browser change-and-restore pairs took 1.52–2.27s; final PNG export and readback took about 0.94s. These conditions do not establish phone or maximum-resolution performance.
